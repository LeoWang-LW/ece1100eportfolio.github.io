About me

I grew up in Gaithersburg, Maryland, and I got into engineering the way a lot of people do: someone handed me a robot kit and I didn't want to put it down.

Robots first

I joined my high school's FIRST Tech Challenge team in ninth grade and ended up leading it. We designed the robot in Onshape and Fusion 360, 3D printed the parts we couldn't buy, and wrote the code that drove it. That team reached the regional semifinals, and the parts I modeled the next year helped us win the THINK Engineering Notebook Award. By senior year I was mentoring the younger members instead of driving.

Science Olympiad taught me a different lesson. In the Robot Tour event, a partner and I built a robot that had to drive itself through a randomized course and stop on a target, as close as possible to a target time. Ours kept drifting off line. I wrote stabilization and course-correction code until it stopped, and we took first place at the UMBC tournament and seventh in Maryland. That was the first time I fixed a hardware problem in software, and it is a big part of why I chose electrical engineering over mechanical.

Why sustainability matters to me

The other half of high school was climate work. I was one of 18 students selected for my county's Student Climate Action Council, which speaks for more than 160,000 students on environmental issues. I helped write sustainability recommendations for the school district and testified before the Board of Education in support of green gardens and permeable surfaces to reduce stormwater runoff. On weekends I collected plastic film with a group called Zero to Hero, which turned more than 1,500 pounds of it into benches for public parks.

Those two halves are the same goal to me. Engineering has always been tied to helping people, and the most useful thing I can build is something that makes technology cleaner.

At Georgia Tech

My first real test here was HyTech Racing, the Formula SAE electric team. To join, I had to design a keychain PCB in Altium, a tool I had never opened, and then write firmware for a temperature-sensing light. I spent a lot of hours teaching myself Altium. Finishing it showed me that I can learn an unfamiliar tool on my own, and now I'm designing a brake light system for the car.

Outside engineering

I swim, and I spent several years teaching kids to swim as an instructor and assistant coach. I ran cross country and track, and I want to run a marathon before I graduate. I co-founded my high school's Model UN club and grew it to more than 40 members. I speak English and Mandarin, and learning piano is on my list.
